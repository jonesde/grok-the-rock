# Plan: Voice pass on `mending-place.html`

## Goal

Reduce second-person address (`you` / `your` / etc.) where it isolates the reader, sounds instructional-at-a-distance, or risks feeling accusatory — so the guide walks *with* the reader without lecturing them.

Success is **justified remainders**, not a low hit count. Many `you`s must stay (Rule 7, personal stewardship, quotes, scripts).

## Voice rules (authoritative)

Apply in this order:

1. **Prefer impersonal structure** when the sentence works without `you` *or* `we`.
   - **Bare imperative first** (warmest impersonal): “Choose one… Reduce… Notice… Name it…”
   - Concrete noun-led: “Empathy still protects the injured…”, “Attendance without converting the table is allowed…”
   - Passive only when imperative or noun-led is awkward: “Protective limits are drawn from what is legitimately stewarded…”
   - Generic third person: “people”, “the injured party”, “a household”, “the body”, “the practicing adult”
   - **Avoid stiff “one / one’s / oneself”** — fights the book’s plain register. Prefer imperative, concrete nouns, or “a person.”
   - Rationale: less personal, less potentially triggering; avoids both lecture-`you` and forced-`we`. Prefer warmth (imperative) over bureaucratic passive.

2. **Use inclusive `we` / `our` only** when it truly means author + reader sharing the work, and does **not** force the author into a claim, decision, or situation that is not shared.
   - Good: shared practice, shared weather of ranking/repair, shared experiments framed as joint discipline.
   - Bad (royal / forced we): putting the author inside the reader’s household, partner, trauma state, property choices, or entitlements.

3. **Keep `you` / `your`** when the matter is a **personal decision** or **personal stake** — especially over one’s own body, dependents, property, stewardships, or what one makes and may open.
   - Canonical keep: Rule 7 title and framing **“Open what you make”** (and body language that is about the maker’s own work/property).
   - Also keep: protective limits over *your* body / *what is yours to tend* when rewriting to impersonal would blur that the line is personal, not collective ownership.
   - Also keep (careful): refusal-section language on **protecting self/dependents** and **spaces under personal control** — personal locus, not collective claim.

4. **Never change**
   - Scripture quotations
   - Quoted speech (good or bad examples)
   - Dialogue / parent / threat-mode **scripts** (the spoken lines)
   - Diagnostic questions already in first-person `I` / `my` (self-check form)

5. **Inclusive we ≠ royal we**  
   If “we” would claim the author is in the reader’s marriage, flooding, refusal dynamics, or property call, restructure impersonally or keep a careful `you` for that personal locus.

## Decision matrix (quick)

| Situation | Prefer |
|-----------|--------|
| General principle, diagnosis, community pattern | Impersonal (imperative / noun-led before passive) |
| Shared practice the book models with the reader | Inclusive `we` (sparingly) |
| Personal property, making, body, stewarded charge, personal yes/no | Keep `you` / `your` |
| Quotes, scripts, scripture, example dialogue | Unchanged |
| Self-exam prompt | Keep `I` / `my` diagnostics; experiments → imperative or light `we` only if joint |

### B. Light inclusive `we` (only where shared and natural)

Use sparingly; Rule 5 already models this well. Good fits:

- Shared noticing of ranking weather (if impersonal feels cold)
- “How do we repair it?” already present — leave
- Paired “our fairness / our compassion…” questions — leave
- Closing maintenance that is clearly communal practice among people using the book **without** claiming the author’s household
- Periodic check “Where did we reduce leverage…” — leave

If in doubt between `we` and impersonal → **impersonal**.

### C. Explicit keep-`you` zones (personal decision / property / body)

Do **not** convert these to `we`/`our`, and do **not** force full impersonal when it blurs personal locus:

- **Open what you make** (title + personal open/share choices)
- Guarding **your** body, dependents, property, stewardships entrusted **to you** (Rule 3 body)
- Standing **your** ground / what is **yours** to tend (personal locus of stand)
- “Tend what is entrusted to you” when it means personal charge
- **Refusal — personal stake:** keep careful `you` on “protect yourself and any dependents,” “reduce the ways in which you feed…,” “spaces you do control” (mix with impersonal non-coercion line from A)
- Experiment items that are inherently private property/skill: prefer imperative first (“Choose one skill that can be safely shared…”); a single `you` is fine if impersonal is awkward

## Register samples (lock tone before bulk edit)

**Opening (A):**  
Before: “If you are reading this in a hard moment, start with the children’s books…”  
After: “In a hard moment, start with the children’s books; this guide waits until there is room to pause and ponder, and it will still be here when it is needed.”

**Refusal (A + C mix):**  
Before: “You are not entitled to force… You are entitled to protect yourself… You are entitled to reduce the ways in which you feed… spaces you do control.”  
After: “No one is entitled to force the rules on another adult. Coercing someone into ‘healing’ reproduces the original violation of agency. You are entitled to protect yourself and any dependents from ongoing harm, and to reduce the ways you feed destructive dynamics. In practice this often means continuing the rules in the spaces you do control…”

**Experiment (A):**  
Before: “Each time you notice the urge to rank yourself above or below someone, pause…”  
After: “Each time the urge arises to rank above or below someone, pause and name the specific small feeling underneath. Do not act on the ranking impulse for sixty seconds. Notice what happens.”

## Success criteria

- Reader feels accompanied, not singled out or accused.
- Personal property and personal protective decisions still use clear `you` where needed (esp. Rule 7, Rule 3 stand/limits, refusal protect/control).
- No royal-we that drafts the author into the reader’s life.
- Where neither person is needed, prose is calm impersonal — warm imperative preferred over cold passive or “one/one’s.”
- Diagnostics stay `I`/`my`; experiments are mostly imperative/impersonal.
- Every remaining `you`/`your` hit is justified under keep rules; hit count alone is not a metric.
- Glossary definitions may be mildly more impersonal than Rule 3 body without contradicting personal stewardship.
